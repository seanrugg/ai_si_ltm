Z3-R0 build model

Build plan v2.1 · 12 Oct 2026. A two-deck 4WD rover built around the UNO Q, with distance sensing across its front arc and behind it, a USB webcam and a Pi Camera for eyes, and an OLED face. It talks to Vector through the robot mesh on the Jetson. An on-board Pi Zero W streams the Pi Camera to the Jetson and relays long-term memory from the memory server to the Q.     Wiring schedule

    The pin map from the build plan, checked against the UNO Q datasheet. D5, D6, D9 and D10 are PWM pins; the left driver's direction lines use A2–A5 so A0/A1 stay free as the Q's only DAC outputs; D2/D3 take the speed sensors on interrupts. D11 (PWM) is spare; the version 2 pan-tilt head uses a PCA9685 servo board on the header I²C bus, so it needs no extra pins. UNO Q PWM runs at a fixed 500 Hz, so expect a faint motor whine at low speeds.

    FromToWireNotes
    Before powering up: never connect the battery directly to the Q's 5V pin. Set the buck converter to 5.1V with a multimeter first. The 5V pin needs a regulated 5V supply that can deliver at least 3A and shares a net with USB-C power, so never plug a charger into the Q or the UGREEN's PD port while the buck is connected. Skip VIN (7–24V): a 2S pack sags below 7V. Tie all grounds together.

    Version 2, pan-tilt head (toggle it above): the Pi Camera moves to a pan-tilt bracket on two micro servos, driven by a PCA9685 on the header I²C bus at 0x40, powered by its own 5V buck with a 100 µF capacitor. Clone builders can skip the Pi Zero and Pi Camera for a webcam-only robot.

    Parts bin after the build: 1 TB6612, 3 LM393 sensors, 4 battery holders, 1 mic, the kit’s 4×AA box, the AIY Vision Bonnet (its Myriad chip no longer boots), and the ESP32 with the TP4056 and ten MT3608 boards (a future handheld remote), plus spare fuses and Qwiic cables.

    Optional later: Pi Zero 2 W upgrade, ReSpeaker mic array (far-field listening), a short USB extension to move the mic up the mast.

    Robot mesh and audit trail

    Every communication, decision and action is recorded in three tiers, and every action carries its outcome. Records are hash-chained, so an edited or missing record breaks the chain. Toggle the Wi-Fi / MQTT layer to see the links.

    TierWhereRoleWrite access
    1. Local journalZ3-R0, SQLite on eMMCZ3-R0's own source of truth; written before acting, forwarded to the mesh, replayed after an outageZ3-R0 only
    2. Mesh governance logJetsonAuthoritative record for every robot and agent on the mesh; verifies each chain and acknowledges recordsMesh logger only
    3. Long-term memoryMemory server (internet or home LAN)History every enrolled mesh member can query; dashboard for the administrator. Z3-R0's Pi Zero reads it and relays relevant records to the QVerified ingest only; read-only for the Pi Zero

    Local retention (defaults to confirm): 2 GB cap, acknowledged records kept 30 days, unacknowledged records never purged, each purge logged as its own record.
